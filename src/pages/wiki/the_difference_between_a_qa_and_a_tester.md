# The Difference Between a QA and a Tester

This question came up in a recent presentation I attend. It's actually a fairly common misconception that the two are the same.

## What is testing?
Testing is an empirical task. Validating the outcome of a test against a set of assumptions. Testers take this a step further by investigating code from an objective point of view, poking around to see if the subject is fit for purpose.

## So how is that different from Quality Assurance
QA is an entirely different, far reaching role. Historically testing has been considered a chore, something to be skipped over and avoided at all costs - developers know best, right? Wrong, citations not required, just look back at your own experience over the last 20-30 years - software fails more often than not.

Agile has pushed and raised the profile of a QA. Testing code is not enough to decide whether it is fit for purpose. Quality must be introduced throughout the software development process. Techniques such as Continuous Integration, Test-Driven-Development & Pair Programming are all examples of quality assurance in action. As role, a QA would aim to facilitate such techniques and validate they are being carried out correctly. 

**He or she is the watchdog of the development process. It is their responsibility to make sure quality is being produced at all levels.**
