## Introduction
For those who do not know, the Libretto was one of the first "Micro-laptops". You could, arguable, consider them one of the first net-books.

This tiny little computers, approximately the size of a VHS cassette come in various specifications ranging from a 486 to a rockin' Pentium. For those adventerous few (including me), various hardware modifications open up a new realm of possibilities. Overclocking these devices is fairly trivial - okay fair enough, for those who know what they are doing, upgrading hard-disks and even the potential for 96MiB ram can be done!

These little laptops are becoming ever more rare but with an eye on ebay and a palm full of gold - there's many great laptops still around!.

### Libretto Vs. Asus EEE 701
The Asus EEE is another of my little passions. It is the first of a modern day breed of micro-laptops. Like the Toshiba a little ahead of it's time but a fantastic device no less. Sadly as time has gone on, the market has driven for larger and less novel devices. The days of 7" laptops, outside of the elite, are numbered.

## The Windows 98 Look for Windows 95
Windows 95 is old, *real old*. Yet for these little laptops the only way you'll get the best out of them is to run an archaic operating system. Linux is possible (more on that later) yet in all honesty, it uses to much memory and is to inflexible for these devices. The terminal is great but it doesn't really convey the power of these devices.

Rather than using Windows 98 - which takes yet more previous resources - you can use Windows 95 with the Internet Explorer 4.0 desktop pack. You should ideally install it before you install a later browser. If you've already installed IE5 or 6 don't despair as you can update, it's just a little harder. I'll post more on this later.

![Windows95 With DesktopPack](/wiki-media/Windows95_With_DesktopPack.JPG)

Once you've installed the desktop pack you'll be able to upgrade to IE5.5 or IE6. I'd suggest upgrading in order or you may run into problems.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Why doesn't my Libretto 50CT charge when I'm using it?** 

Turns out that the battery charging circuit is actually separate from the main circuit. You have to power it down before it'll charge - go figure.

**What type of battery charger does the Libretto 50CT require?**

TBC. Unlike the 100 series, it uses a cylindrical plug. The 50CT requires a PA2500U AC Adapter which outputs 15V (at 2Amps)

**How can I find out how long my Libretto's OS has been installed?**

Check out Aida32. You can grab a shareware copy from [here](http://download.milesburton.com/Libretto/Misc/aida32pe_393.zip)
