First up, let me say how hard this scheme was from start to finish. As of 2009, the scheme went: On-line Application [CV], SHL Test, Telephone Interview, Assessment Day 1, Assessment Day 2.

### Assessment Day 2
The final assessment day was pretty gruelling! I skipped the group activities as I was not available when the other interviewees were assessed.

The day went in this order:
Presentation
Interview (not technical)
SHL 'Fast Track' cards

#### The Presentation
A little naughty but I snuck out the preparation sheet I was given. I don't recall signing an NDA, I'll claim ignorance for your benefit.

Preparation time: 30 minutes
Presentation time: 15-20 minutes
Materials: Flip chart, pens, confidence

''"Describe a recent software development project or piece of coursework that you have done and outline the following aspects of your work.

1. The purpose of the project, who was involved, and any challenges it posed.

2. The process you used to develop the software.

3. Any specific problems you came across and how you overcame them

4. How you went about testing your work

5. The outcome of your project

6. Any learning points from your work."
''

From the feedback I received, try and make this reasonably technically focussed (though don’t go mad). Make sure you outline the project fully.

#### The Interview
I found the interview itself to be quite a pleasure. The project manager (your future boss) and the HR lady to be very nice and keen to listen.

Much to my distaste (as a candidate), they use a very 2 dimensional reviewing scheme. You are graded, per question, on a scale of 1-5 (5 is good). I would argue that such a scale does not take into account your real-world experience and the potential for alternate "out-of-the-box" answers. My advice to any future candidates is: when you answer, be specific, don't provide "out-of-the-box" answers. Do NOT skip a question if you can avoid it, I believe it will count against you.

The interview wasn't overly technical, a few questions will likely be thrown in for the managers own interest and will give you a chance to separate yourself from the crowd. The interview questions are mostly "tell me a time when..." (the name alludes me now, begins with c :)).

Most were pretty standard. 
"Tell me a time when you overran a project deadline"
"What was your role within a team"
"Tell me about a time when you were successful"

#### SHL Cards

Without going into to much detail, you have a whole bunch of cards. On the cards there's various information. A couple of the cards will have missing information which you'll need to "fill-in" by using the other cards.

A quick example of a few of them are:
**Teleconference** 
You have to arrange a video-conferencing meeting with participants from all over the world. Each of the cards contained a previous meeting and their associated times in GMT and local. With a calculator handy, you'd muddle through them and find an answer :)

**The Library**
Very much the same as above, each card has a book, an associated category, price, price-code. 

You had to work out what the missing cards were

**The Taxman**
Various tax codes and prices. 

**The Flights**
Many price codes for places around the world. Sydney to England at a certain class, ie business, would have a cost.
