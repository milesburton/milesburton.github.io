## Introduction
As developers enter the market they are usually very keen to absorb everything they can get their hands on, any tit-bit of information, any golden nugget becomes a building block for all future work. I believe one of the major problems most developers suffer is a technical lock-in. As time passes they become comfortable with their way with less and less consideration for the outside world. I make no excuses as I've fallen into the same trap, your way works right? Why change it? 

The real art of development and business growth is strive to forever improve and expand your understanding. Never, ever take what you know for granted. Ask questions, investigate concepts which are outside of your comfort zone and view the world for a different perspective. 

...and no this doesn't mean biting off more than you can chew. Like everything in life you change a little here, change a little there. Ever improving, ever evolving. You'll find sudden jumps in productivity as you "get it". You're no longer developing a piece of code but trying to improve your business - earn your payslip. 

It's very easy to become detached from the very purpose you write code, the business need. 

You'll notice my "reading lists" are less focussed on technical prowess and esoteric languages but on the "why", the purpose. It's all about approaching problems from a different perspective :).

## The Books!
- [How would you move Mount Fiji?](http://www.amazon.com/Would-Move-Mount-Microsofts-Puzzle/dp/0316919160) - A book spanning back to the Heydays of Microsoft. It's filled with interesting puzzles aimed at "Lateral Thinking" which were originally used in Microsoft interviews. It's a fantastic book and you often see the questions pop up time to time (3 pirates, 5 gold coins)

- [Extreme-Programming - Kent Beck](http://www.amazon.com/Extreme-Programming-Explained-Embrace-Change/dp/0321278658/ref=sr_1_1?ie=UTF8&s=books&qid=1265112038&sr=1-1) - A great book for understanding the concept of Extreme Programming and what it aims to achieve

- Head-first Software Development - A fantastic introduction to Agile development (Reading between the lines, it appears to refer to Scrum). Unlike most books it's a fascinating read which is very hard to put down despite being quite in-depth. Highly recommended for an Agile recap or those who've always followed predictive methodologies.

- Domain Driven Design - I'm in two minds about this book, it covers some very interesting topics but it's quite a tough read. Unlike other books I've mentioned in this article it doesn't jump out at you quite so much. Instead it seems to be targeted at intermediate developers and managers who already have a firm grasp of Object Oriented Programming and Design Patterns.

- Head-first Design Patterns - You've got to love O'Reilly and their Head-First Series. Unlike the original it provides practical step by step examples of why you'd use certain design patterns and how they are useful. It's a great light hearted look at design patterns but more seasoned developers may find it lacking in depth.

- [Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software](http://www.amazon.com/Design-Patterns-Elements-Reusable-Object-Oriented/dp/0201633612/ref=sr_1_1?ie=UTF8&s=books&qid=1265112327&sr=1-1) - No reading list could be complete without this book. It's required reading full stop. It covers a huge range of patterns which you *will* come across and use time and time again. Get it, refer to it, read it.
