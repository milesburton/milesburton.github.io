# Why Your Personal Projects Fail

Time and time again I hear about these fantastic ideas that never get off the ground. Friends & developers who are much better engineers than I simply cannot get an idea out the door.

Why? Actually no, we all know why. Ideas become so grandiose that even the thought of writing that first line sends chills down your spine. You know you should break it down but you can't. Even when your hacking away you just *have* to implement that object with AOP, those constants *need* to be in a DSL.

... No, no they don't. **Stop.**

## No, just no
When you start *any* project do not get carried away. Do the bare minimum first, use hacks. Put your file paths directly in your code. Leave AOP.

It is exactly this which stops you from progressing. Your blank canvas has just doubled in size. You've just dug a little deeper into a rabbit hole that you're not even sure you want to go down. 

## Embrace agile, just not all of it
The psychological effect of having 'something' is very powerful. No longer are you without context, boundaries. You've made progress. With this progress you can begin the process of re-factoring. Taking little bits of code and implement new features, re-factoring and getting your testing up to scratch.

You can see this time and time again. You're writing a presentation staring at 10 blank slides. Throw some bullet points down. Go from there, isn't that much easier? 

## Counter arguments
I often hear so many arguments against this technique. Many are self defeating.

- I need to do it right, first time.

Why? The more you write code the better you'll get. Through the process of re-factoring you'll become better and faster at writing those exotic techniques. You'll become so good infact you'll begin with that code allowing you to advance ever faster.

- What if someone sees this 'terrible' code?

**Embrace bad**. I've yet to hear anyone complain that a simple prototype had poor code. A prototype is exactly that, a work in progress. Its only purpose is to get better. If you're really that concerned add comments highlighting you are aware of the quality and have a plan.

## What to take away
- Do the bare minimum first - Keep away from implementing other ideas just yet. Keep away from that rabbit hole.
- Embrace bad code - You're developing a prototype which will grow and continually improve.
- Working software over perfection - Perfect code which never sees the light of day is useless. A working prototype is ready for improvement.
