<p>Finding great developers can often feel like the search for the holy grail. Each developer is an individual and they are their own strengths and weaknesses. Some developers relish the idea of an abstract question which they can hammer till completion whilst others are pragmatic and want to see the AC and drill their way to the solution.</p>
<p>Interviewing is an art and, you need to weigh several factors before you even stand a chance to find out if they are your &#8216;rockstar&#8217;.</p>
<p>When I look for new developers I find this three stage process works best:</p>
<ul>
<li><span style="line-height:14px;">15 minute &#8216;fizzbuzz&#8217; [or coin, TBC] test &#8211; the object is to determine can they actually write code </span></li>
<li>Short interview (45min) to determine team fit</li>
<li>1.5 hour simple &#8216;pairing&#8217; test &#8211; Do they work well with others? Are their answers logical (Sorting a list? Which Java interfaces did they use?)</li>
</ul>
<p>&#8211; If you have no intention of hiring a candidate make-sure they are excused as soon as possible. Do not waste their time.</p>
<p>Outside interests are often worth much more than our short interview can uncover. Consider the following:</p>
<ul>
<li>Public code &#8211; good use of tests and/or intuitive thinking &#8211; (4 stars)</li>
<li>Twitter &#8211; What is your candidate reading? (3 stars)</li>
<li>Blogging &#8211; Time permitting what are they introducing to the software craftsman community? (5 stars)</li>
<li>User groups/meetups &#8211; are they involved in the community? What are their interests? &#8211; (4 stars)</li>
</ul>
<p>&#8211; Don&#8217;t be to hash though, time is precious and each of these points takes a large time investment. </p>
