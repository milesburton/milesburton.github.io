<p>While the gamers of the early nighties were tearing their hair out &#8211; and no doubt their wallets. Thankfully several little gems have remained for us today and one of which is the ever-rare Amstad Mega PC.</p>
<p>If you&#8217;re not familiar with the Mega PC, it was a standard IBM compatible which had a cheeky little sliding door that switched it between Windows and gaming nirvana; Inside it&#8217;s seemly innocent exterior was a red blooded Mega Drive in the form of a modestly sized ISA card which provided full RGB output and adlib compatible audio. All this could be had for the low, low price of £599 (+VAT) or nearly £1316.15 in today&#8217;s money.</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" data-attachment-id="1136" data-permalink="/blog/upgrading-the-amstrad-mega-pc/" data-orig-file="/blog-media/2016/01/megapc.jpg" data-orig-size="960,540" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;0&quot;}" data-image-title="megapc" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-large-file="/blog-media/2016/01/megapc.jpg" class=" size-full wp-image-1136 aligncenter" src="/blog-media/2016/01/megapc.jpg" alt="megapc" width="960" height="540" srcset="/blog-media/2016/01/megapc.jpg 960w, /blog-media/2016/01/megapc.jpg 150w, /blog-media/2016/01/megapc.jpg 300w, /blog-media/2016/01/megapc.jpg 768w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 960px) 100vw, 960px" /></p>
<p>With our modern Octo-core smart phones and <a href="http://www.bbc.co.uk/news/business-34993205">exploding hoverboards</a> why am I so curious about this humble little 386, significantly outclassed even in 1993? Who know&#8217;s, maybe my British desire to be both quietly conservative, yet cause a little chaos and fun at the same time &#8230;And who doesn&#8217;t want a bit more fun?</p>
<p>Quite surprising, yet the Mega PC is rife for upgrading. It is possible to upgrade almost anything, from the mundane memory upgrade, through to an full CPU turbocharge to a 486!</p>
<h2>So what&#8217;s a nice easy upgrade?</h2>
<h3>Math Coprocessor</h3>
<p>Well the easiest upgrade is the 387DX.</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" data-attachment-id="1195" data-permalink="/blog/upgrading-the-amstrad-mega-pc/" data-orig-file="/blog-media/2016/01/intel387dx.jpg" data-orig-size="600,451" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;0&quot;}" data-image-title="intel387dx" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-large-file="/blog-media/2016/01/intel387dx.jpg" class="  wp-image-1195 aligncenter" src="/blog-media/2016/01/intel387dx.jpg" alt="intel387dx" width="220" height="165" srcset="/blog-media/2016/01/intel387dx.jpg 220w, /blog-media/2016/01/intel387dx.jpg 440w, /blog-media/2016/01/intel387dx.jpg 150w, /blog-media/2016/01/intel387dx.jpg 300w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 220px) 100vw, 220px" /></p>
<p>It&#8217;s a Math Coprocessor which will give a few selected apps a bit of a boost &#8211; sadly, it wont give a boost unless a specific app or game requires it (<a href="http://www.retrocityrampage.com/msdos_retail.php">RetroCity</a>!), but you do get a nice bit of e-bling in Windows 95.</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" data-attachment-id="1208" data-permalink="/blog/upgrading-the-amstrad-mega-pc/80387/" data-orig-file="/blog-media/2016/01/80387.png" data-orig-size="554,580" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;0&quot;}" data-image-title="80387" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-large-file="/blog-media/2016/01/80387.png" class=" size-full wp-image-1208 aligncenter" src="/blog-media/2016/01/80387.png" alt="80387.png" width="554" height="580" srcset="/blog-media/2016/01/80387.png 554w, /blog-media/2016/01/80387.png 143w, /blog-media/2016/01/80387.png 287w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 554px) 100vw, 554px" /></p>
<p>It&#8217;s pretty easy to find on eBay. There are a ton of variants which are pin compatible with the empty slot on your MegaPC &#8211; most of which are a less than $10.</p>
<h3>Ethernet</h3>
<p>With this technology fastly approaching 30 years old, ISA cards are becoming absolute gold dust. That said, their relative simplicity compared to modern standards means they tend to be fairly reliable.</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" data-attachment-id="2037" data-permalink="/blog/upgrading-the-amstrad-mega-pc/" data-orig-file="/blog-media/2019/01/download.jpeg" data-orig-size="259,194" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;0&quot;}" data-image-title="download" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-large-file="/blog-media/2019/01/download.jpeg" class=" size-full wp-image-2037 aligncenter" src="/blog-media/2019/01/download.jpeg" alt="download" width="259" height="194" srcset="/blog-media/2019/01/download.jpeg 259w, /blog-media/2019/01/download.jpeg 150w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 259px) 100vw, 259px" /></p>
<p>Whilst the MegaPC will never be a Reddit browsing machine, adding an Ethernet card makes it vastly easier to add retro games and software &#8211; just bare in mind it&#8217;ll add an overhead to the box. I&#8217;d also recommend sticking with Windows 95 which has oddly quite good support for ISA.</p>
<h3>Memory</h3>
<p>Before we start stepping into the realms of modern upgrades, maxing out the amount of memory your MegaPC can support is a given. The Mega PC used 30 pin SIMM RAM, and unfortunately it&#8217;s tricky to find the correct modules to match the machine &#8211; memory frequency/timing differs with various modules, finding the a good match should give allow you to upgrade to 16Meg.</p>

<h3>Going hardcore, the 486 clip on upgrade</h3>
<p>Rare as the proverbial hen&#8217;s tooth it is possible to upgrade your Mega PC from it&#8217;s 386SX 25Mhz to a gluttonous, number crunching 486 using a rather unusual clip-on upgrade.</p>
<p>With much sadness I never managed to get hold of this particular upgrade myself, yet a lucky few have managed to secure the Citrix CX486SRX. This piece of clever engineering clips on top of your existing 386 providing a fairly substantial boost to performance &#8211; just bare in mind you are still restricted to a 16 bit bus, and yes, you still want that coprocessor.</p>
