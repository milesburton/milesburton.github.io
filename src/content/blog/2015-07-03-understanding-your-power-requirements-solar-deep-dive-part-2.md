<p>Before you consider either project it’s best to find out what your base load actually is. If you’re not familiar with base load (BL) it essentially means the power usage which is always drawn from your power supply. In your home, something like a fridge will draw a continuous amount of power which would contribute to your BL. In the UK the average base load is roughly 250-300 watts. If you intend to supplement your home power, products such as <a href="https://engage.efergy.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Efergy’s Engage</a> can be particularly useful as it can clamp around your incoming supply.</p>
<p>In a shed/outbuilding project a simple <a href="http://www.amazon.co.uk/gp/product/B00E4H0L40/ref=s9_simh_bw_p60_d0_i2?pf_rd_m=A3P5ROKL5A1OLE&amp;pf_rd_s=merchandised-search-9&amp;pf_rd_r=1GB57FDRTFAZDK257MD8&amp;pf_rd_t=101&amp;pf_rd_p=628716127&amp;pf_rd_i=1938287031">Watt-Meter</a> (around £10) combined with a standard extension lead can allow you to estimate your power use during an average day.</p>
<p>The reason BL is useful is it can be used to estimate the amount of solar power you actually need. If you continuously need 300Watts, then a 600-800Watt array would be suitably sized* as a minimum.</p>
<p><i>*Speaking of which&#8230;</i></p>
<h4>&#8230;Location, location, location</h4>
<p>The location and angle of your panel combined with the season all have a major part to play in the efficiency of your system. Consider the angle of the panel, in the summer here in London is 51 degrees should be the optimal position, however in winter that angle needs to flatten somewhat as the axis of the planet moves further away from the sun. I won’t go into too much detail here, as <a href="http://www.uksolarenergy.org.uk/installing-solar-panels.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">UK Solar Energy have covered it quite well</a>.</p>
<p>As a first step however, consider the location of your house and work out the best direction to point your panels to harness the most sun during the day. Suncalc has produced a <a href="http://suncalc.net/#/51.5,-0.117,12/2015.07.03/14:22" target="_blank" rel="noopener">very handy map which will assist you</a></p>
