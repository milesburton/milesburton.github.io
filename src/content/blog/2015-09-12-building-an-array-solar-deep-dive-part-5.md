<p>The Winter solstice maybe baring down, yet what a great opportunity to give you a run-down on my build and how the darker days will impact your yield.</p>
<p>My first foray into solar started with a humble 120W/12V panel, coupled with a rather dodgy PWM charge controller. With any new hobby, start humbly and cheap :). The initial plan was can a security camera be powered purely from Solar?</p>
<p>On paper the camera drew around .6amp at 220 volts. Significantly lower than the 10amps the 120 watt panel could, at least in theory, produce. Funnily enough, it did rather well. All I needed was a new shed &#8211; ideally without Asbestos.</p>
<p>Buying a shed isn&#8217;t exactly lifes most exciting task that said, if you are interested in Solar &#8211; get a Pent roof. And with that in mind I bought a nice 8&#215;6 :).</p>
<p>With my new man-cave build, I was able to setup a dedicated area for Solar. My new plan, if a touch ambitious, was to go entirely off-grid. I&#8217;d take 6 security cameras coupled with a NAS and run them off the array.</p>
<p>Clearly my dodgy Chinese charge controller simply wasn&#8217;t up to the job. It was time to have a think how I could build a salable grid.</p>
<p>Time to go on a shopping trip.</p>
<ul>
<li>Victron BlueSolar MPPT 30/100</li>
<li>Victron 750/1500 Inverter</li>
<li>120Ah Leisure battery &#8211; a total PoS from autocarpartrus (who&#8217;s customer service runs out faster than his battery)</li>
<li>Lots and lots of 16MM cable</li>
<li>A handful of 30 and 100amp DC RCDs</li>
<li>Thick chipboard!</li>
</ul>
<p>This was quite a jump from my 300 watt modified sine-wave inverter, coupled with a 11Ah knackered AGM battery and the worlds most dodgy PWM controller.</p>
