<p>The Toshiba Libretto broke entirely new ground. The brand, &#8216;Netbook&#8217; was nothing more than a fantasy for Psion. After producing some of the best PDAs they were never able to capture the X86 market. Back in the early 90&#8217;s laptops were huge lumbering machines, a slave to the city trader. With Windows 95 just around the corner a new comer, Toshiba, had arrived. The unthinkable became reality , they had converted the very best of the X86 world into an entirely uncompromised, hand-held format.  The Libretto 20 was born.</p>
<p>These hand-held laptops surpassed the exceptional Psion handhelds of the era. Rather than taking a seriously lacking and and unfamiliar system they took the very best Intel could provide and combined it with a revolutionary OS which was Windows.</p>
<p>Even now you cannot help but be humbled by Toshiba&#8217;s engineering prowess. Fitting such an enormous amount of power into footprint of a VHS cassette tape.</p>
<p>So you&#8217;re probably wondering why this little history lesson appeared. Well these guys unknowingly produced a platform which was exceptionally future proof. We can now take those old boring spinning disks and replace it with the very best solid-state storage Sandisk can produce 😉</p>
<p>Why replace the internal disk at all? Well as the years go by those disks are becoming harder and harder to get and quite frankly, they always sucked. Today&#8217;s compact flash cards provide an access time which a 2.5&#8243; disk could only dream of.  Boot times drop significantly and there&#8217;s no more worrying grinding whilst it tries to read a far-flung file. All of this aside, were not not even considering the sheer storage available today, 4, 8, 16, 32+ GiBs of space. Oh and finally you&#8217;re battery has probably already bitten the dust, if it hasn&#8217;t &#8211; guess how much power a CF card uses 😉</p>
<p>Enough reminiscing.. Let&#8217;s get cracking. First up, compact flash uses an IDE interface. It is quite literally pin compatible with your libretto, all you need is a small adapter.</p>
<p>So let&#8217;s hit eBay. Here&#8217;s your shopping list:</p>
<p>* <a href="http://www.ebay.co.uk/itm/320942895708?ssPageName=STRK:MEWNX:IT&amp;_trksid=p3984.m1439.l2649">Compact Flash CF to 44 Pin 2.5&#8243; IDE(Male) Adaptor Converter Card</a></p>
<p>* <a href="http://www.amazon.co.uk/gp/product/B0007QU6ZQ/ref=oh_details_o08_s00_i00?ie=UTF8&amp;psc=1">SanDisk SDCFH-004G-U46 4GB Ultra 30MB/s CompactFlash Memory Card</a></p>
<p>This mod doesn&#8217;t require much. The Toshiba engineers made sure the Libretto could support a wide range of hard disks so upgrading is almost a drop-in replacement. You only need to do one minor modification, cut the pin key (this is used to make sure you don&#8217;t accidentally plug in the cable the wrong way around).</p>

<p>As you can see from the above image, there are two rows of pins. The bottom row needs to have one pin removed. This pin is the 13th pin from the left and the tenth pin from the right.<br />
When this pin is removed it should fit flush with the IDE slot in the Libretto.</p>

<p>If you purchase the same CF adapter as I did, you may need to cut the master/slave jumper pins to get a snug fit into the hard-disk bay. You can safely cut about half of the length off. Make sure you return the jumper to master mode.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>

<p>The next step is rather fiddly and will require patience. Insert the CF card and adapter face down (the CF card label should be facing down). Insert this into the hard-disk bay and carefully align the pins and apply pressure till the adapter beds correctly into the Libretto.</p>
<p>Once you have secured the adapter into the bay you can boot the Libretto. It&#8217;s worth going into the BIOS to double check everything is as expected. To do this you need to <a href="http://www.notanon.com/retro/how-to-enter-the-bios-on-a-toshiba-libretto-50ct/2011/03/10/">boot up and restart </a>whilst holding down the Escape key &#8211; this is quite fiddly and takes few attempts.</p>
