<p>Just recently I&#8217;ve been branching out on my reading material, widening my technical scope to everything from NLP, Consulting to Project Management. I suppose it&#8217;s to be expected but it still takes me by surprise that an entirely different field, for example Health can have such stark overlaps with IT.</p>
<p>Whilst reading &#8220;<a href="http://www.amazon.co.uk/The-Secrets-Consulting-Getting-Successfully/dp/0932633013">The Secrets of Consulting</a>&#8221; I spotted this great analogy reinforcing the old cliché, &#8220;Scope, Schedule &amp; Cost  you can pick any two&#8221; (the old iron triangle). The author imagined a scenario where a client asked for the fastest runner.</p>
<blockquote><p>&#8220;Let me check my trade off chart&#8221;</p>
<p>1: &#8220;What distance do you wish to work with?</p>
<p>2: &#8220;It will be relatively easy to get a system that runs fast at 100 meters, if it doesn&#8217;t ever have to run 30,000 meters very well&#8221;</p>
<p>3: &#8220;We can easily find an off-the-shelf system which will lower the cost, but if you want a world record performance, there aren&#8217;t going to be many candidates at any distance.&#8221;</p></blockquote>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="color:#545454;">Courtesy </span>of <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Human_speed_distance_portrait.png">Wikipedia</a> take a look at the maximum human speed per distance. An excellent justification of the hard boundaries within the iron triangle.</p>
