<p>Solar is gaining increasing momentum across the world and, even with the weather, Great Britain. As it becomes increasingly accessible, owing to the ever falling cost per kilowatt hour it is now viable to setup surprisingly powerful arrays for a few hundred pounds.</p>
<h4>A little background: Grid Tied Vs. Off Grid?</h4>
<p>There are two main approaches to Solar energy, Grid-Tied (GT) and Off-Grid installations. Both are tantalizingly interesting for a DIY project being both cheap, and on the face of it, quite simple to build. Unfortunately, and here comes the but – They can be incredibly dangerous, particularly GT solutions.</p>
<h5>So what’s a Grid-Tied system?</h5>
<p>In a traditional house your power supply is provided by your local energy company through the National Grid. It doesn’t matter what time of day or night, you can flip on a switch and electricity is conveniently delivered to your home. Unfortunately, each time we flip that switch it costs us a little money, and if generated by fossil fuels, damaging the environment.</p>
<p>Surprising as it may be, the sun only shines during the day which means you can only generate solar power when the sun is out-<i>*<b>groan</b>*</i>. All is not lost though! In a GT system your solar panels are connected, via an inverter to the National Grid. This is ideal because you can supplement your own power consumption, and if you generate more than you use you can actually sell this back to the grid with a <a href="http://www.energysavingtrust.org.uk/domestic/feed-tariff-scheme" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Feed-In-Tariff scheme</a>.</p>
<h5>Off-Grid? Tin foil what?</h5>
<p>So how does this differ from an Off-Grid system? Unfortunately storing electrical energy is inefficient, short-termed and expensive [Ed: Though Elon Musk is <a href="http://www.wired.com/2015/05/tesla-batteries/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">working on it</a>]. As we’ll see later Off-Grid systems need batteries and charge controllers each of which loose a not insignificant quantity of energy at each junction.</p>
<p>Typically a GT system comprises of two main components, a Solar Array and an Inverter – Not much! An Off-Grid system however adds another two components, a charge controller and a battery bank, neither of which are particularly cheap.<u></u><u></u></p>
<p>GT systems almost entirely bypass this problem by selling your excess power during the day, and buying energy during the night from the Grid.</p>
<p>The real key difference from a Do-It-Yourself perspective is regulation. An Off-Grid solution can be setup almost anywhere without any formal certification whilst GT is significantly more involved and, in most cases, requires a MCS installer – somewhat of a show stopper!</p>
