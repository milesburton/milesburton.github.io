<p><em>(This article was originally written for a local publication)</em></p>
<p><span style="font-weight:400;">Electric Vehicles are a genuinely controversial and equally fascinating topic, over the last twenty years there has been countless headlines, everything from Top Gear staging an apparent </span><a href="https://www.theguardian.com/media/2013/mar/05/top-gear-tesla-jeremy-clarkson"><span style="font-weight:400;">Tesla Roadster supercar breakdown</span></a><span style="font-weight:400;"> to Elon Musk blasting it&#8217;s successor into </span><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk%27s_Tesla_Roadster"><span style="font-weight:400;">space</span></a><span style="font-weight:400;">! </span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;">For reasons I still to this day can&#8217;t understand EVs attract highly polarising opinions, On one hand they are supposedly going to cause a </span><a href="https://www.autocar.co.uk/car-news/new-cars/rapid-ev-take-could-cause-blackouts-national-grid"><span style="font-weight:400;">country wide blackouts</span></a><span style="font-weight:400;"> whilst barely getting you to the shops and back, right through to the unicorn farting panacea that is perfectly green, vegan, tree-hugging motoring &#8211;  both of which are basically nonsense.</span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;">Whilst EVs may seem new, this couldn&#8217;t be further from the truth. The very first electric car can be traced all the way back to the late 1800&#8217;s where France &amp; the UK battled it out to build several prototypes demonstrating the new technology &#8211; a legacy we still use today, the London Underground. </span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;">Early EVs, like today&#8217;s mainstream cars had vastly more torque and power than their counterpart gasoline vehicles, yet they simply couldn&#8217;t compete on energy density &#8211; simply put Petrol &amp; Diesel were plentiful, cheaper and global warming wasn&#8217;t a thing.</span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;">Let&#8217;s fast forward, if you think Tesla or Nissan are bleeding edge technology what if I told you we&#8217;re over 20 years behind the curve! Back in the late 90&#8217;s General Motors in the US produced the very first viable consumer EV &#8211; the aptly named </span><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors_EV1"><span style="font-weight:400;">GM EV1</span></a><span style="font-weight:400;">. This vehicle is fast by today&#8217;s standards 0-60 was under 8 seconds, the range around 100 miles with a fully digital display! </span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Killed_the_Electric_Car%3F"><span style="font-weight:400;">Who Killed the Electric Car</span></a><span style="font-weight:400;">, a film produced in the mid 2000&#8217;s, paints a tantalising picture of a motor industry that not only had produced a viable electric car, but also wanted to destroy it. GM, despite building a car far beyond it&#8217;s time, did everything to stop it succeeding &#8211; they intentionally dissuaded customers from purchasing the car, remove it from forecourts and wouldn&#8217;t even allow you to buy it, lease only. </span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;">The forced lease is the pivot point in the story, GM, having seen how successful the EV1 had become was terrified it would detract from their main profit centres  &#8211; which not long after required a HUGE bailout from the US gov &#8211; they recalled ALL EV1 cars to be crushed. To this day only a handful exist, deactivated, in museums. An extraordinarily sad tale that has pushed back the industry by decades.  </span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;">All was not doom and gloom, a very small footnote can be added &#8211; a small startup called Tesla was founded. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight:400;">When I was a child my friends and I would do nothing but talk about the latest super car, the Jaguar XJ220, McLaren F1 &#8211; we&#8217;d play Top Trumps learning every intimate detail of the Ford Cosworth. </span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;">Today is a far different story, insanely expensive fuel, insurance and rampant breeding of speed cameras have significantly doused the excitement of our kids, what&#8217;s the point one kid told me? How sad I thought to myself, particularly as a motorcyclist &#8211; another dying breed (bloody corners). </span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight:400;">Last week a friend electrified (I&#8217;m sorry) my inner 5 year old. After taking his young daughter to school they were absolutely fascinated about the car, his daughter suddenly became the cool kid. A little bird tells me they are busy googling all the latest Jaguars/Teslas/Nissans and they know all the stats &#8211; how fantastic. </span><span style="font-weight:400;"><br />
</span></p>
