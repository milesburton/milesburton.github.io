<p>Fresh from last nights London Software Craftmanship (codemanship) meet up I thought I&#8217;d share an excellent coding Kata which really gets you thinking.</p>
<ul>
<li>an elevator responds to calls containing a source floor and direction</li>
<li>an elevator delivers passengers to requested floors</li>
<li>elevator calls are queued not necessarily FIFO</li>
<li>you may validate passenger floor requests</li>
<li>you may implement current floor monitor</li>
<li>you may implement direction arrow</li>
<li>you may implement doors (opening and closing)</li>
<li>you may implement DING!</li>
<li>there can be more than one elevator</li>
</ul>
<div></div>
<div>The exercise is deceptively simple, following strict &#8216;Clean Code&#8217; standards (yes that does mean TDD!) try and implement your solution. As always there is no right answer.</div>
<div></div>
<div>My tips:</div>
<div>
<ul>
<li>Do the absolute minimum, always</li>
<li>Don&#8217;t touch any logic implementation until you have a failing test (not compiling is failing)</li>
<li>Red, Green, Refactor</li>
<li>Don&#8217;t fear the notepad, it often helps to draw little diagrams. Throw in a sequence&#8230; Could be garbage, could be gold</li>
<li>If pairing, switch keyboards. It&#8217;s OK to be wrong, you wont get far before you&#8217;re back on track and refactoring your way to the answer.</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div></div>
<div>We spent approximately 2 hours the Kata which given discussion time isn&#8217;t enough! That said it had the creative juices flowing.</div>
