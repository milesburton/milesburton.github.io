<p>This is one of those odd occasions where I&#8217;m both impressed and furious at the same time. Now 2016 is thankfully behind us I felt it was time for a bit of a clear out. Some bits and bobs went on <a href="http://freecycle.org">freecycle</a>, other more valuable equipment went on eBay.</p>
<p>Yesterday I received a rather ludicrous <em>buy in now </em>offer &#8211; sadly a common problem. This particular person was simultaneously and unashamedly reselling older equipment for significantly higher than his &#8220;offer&#8221;. <p>Clearly this individual wanted some sort of revenge. Earlier today I receive a rather<br />
unusual text on my company mobile &#8211; supposedly eBay were restricting my account.</p>
<p><em>&#8230;odd, why would eBay have my business mobile?</em></p>
<p>Regardless, it&#8217;s always wise to perform a little due-diligence before clicking any links or attachments in emails &#8211; text messages are no different <em>[&#8230;and interestingly, phone calls but that&#8217;s a story for another day. ed]</em>.</p>
<p>After checking my eBay account for any messages or otherwise (nothing found), I thought to check out the domain in the text.  I performed a <a href="https://who.is/whois/tr.im">WHOIS</a> on the domain in the text message &#8211; that&#8217;s funny, nothing related to eBay.</p>
<p>Another little gem was spotted &#8211; the query string parameter <em>uname</em> is actually a base64 encoded version of my eBay username. If it were indeed eBay that&#8217;s very sloppy.</p>

<p>That&#8217;s a pretty damn good clone of eBay! Let me login&#8230;</p>
<p><span style="color:#000000;"><em><strong>Oh noes! </strong></em></span>Let me immediately put in all my credit card details &#8211; <em>I think not.</em></p>
<p>What&#8217;s interesting about the domain you&#8217;d never believe it wasn&#8217;t registered by <a href="https://who.is/whois/tr.im">eBay</a></p>
<h2>So what next?</h2>
<p>Whether the first url was legitimate, the second most definitely is not.</p>
<p>Both domains were reported to their respective registrars, and eBay was sent a transcript and timeline of everything that happened.</p>
<p>I must admit my hacker mentality wants to deep a little deeper. Maybe dig up some fake credit card details that&#8217;ll pass their validation &#8211; but we&#8217;ll leave that for the fraud departments.</p>
<h2>How does this work?</h2>
<p>If I were a betting man I would imagine this phishing scheme is some sort of &#8220;rent-revenge&#8221; service. I&#8217;ve come across quite a few similar schemes over there years where you pay using alternative methods <em>[that would likely be bitcoin today. ed].</em></p>
<h2>The big ?</h2>
<p>Whilst those who are savvy to such social-engineering attacks may have spotted this a mile away &#8211; the real question is how did they get my name and number?</p>
<p>As a general rule of thumb I avoid sharing personal, and social media details online. Yet somehow this person was able to discover my name from a meaningless eBay username, and then somehow uncover my private business phone.</p>
<p>I&#8217;m continuing to dig. My guess would be a previous eBay leak contained some of my partial details and the rest was carried out through various google searches &#8211; who knows? I&#8217;ll definitely take even more care in future.</p>
<p><strong>How to Protect Yourself from Phishing </strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Never <em><strong>EVER </strong></em>click on any links until you have validated they are genuine through alternative means. For example, if you receive a text from your bank &#8211; log into your bank website and check if you have any messages.</li>
<li>Does it look obvious? I would imagine eBay would use a eBay style link shortener.</li>
<li><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-factor_authentication">Enable two factor authentication </a>on anything you use online</li>
<li>Consider using different emails or alias&#8217; for each service that you use. It&#8217;ll be more difficult to perform a database match if all your details are different</li>
<li>If in doubt. Walk away.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Conclusion</h2>
<p>A great fun diversion for a quiet Saturday afternoon, and yet it&#8217;s worrying in itself. Phishing attacks have moved on significantly since the <a href="http://www.419eater.com/">419</a> days and everyone needs to be on guard.</p>
<p>Whether this was some sort of revenge from an eBay user or a data breach I suppose we&#8217;ll never know. At least it&#8217;s good blog material.</p>
