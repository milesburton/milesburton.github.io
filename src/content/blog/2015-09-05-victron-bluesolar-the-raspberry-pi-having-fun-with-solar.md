<p>Victron, based in Germany, have gained a significant amount of respect within the hobbyist and professional circles over the last few years. After poking around a number of MPPT charge controllers, my heart was set on the BlueSolar 100/30.</p>
<p>What frustrated me a<a href="/blog-media/2015/09/416694933_894.jpg"><img loading="lazy" data-attachment-id="1039" data-permalink="/blog/victron-bluesolar-the-raspberry-pi-having-fun-with-solar/" data-orig-file="/blog-media/2015/09/1407316419_upload_documents_1600_640-bluesolar-mppt-100-30_left.jpg" data-orig-size="960,640" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;0&quot;}" data-image-title="1407316419_upload_documents_1600_640-BlueSolar-MPPT-100-30_left" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-large-file="/blog-media/2015/09/1407316419_upload_documents_1600_640-bluesolar-mppt-100-30_left.jpg" class=" wp-image-1039 alignleft" src="/blog-media/2015/09/1407316419_upload_documents_1600_640-bluesolar-mppt-100-30_left.jpg" alt="1407316419_upload_documents_1600_640-BlueSolar-MPPT-100-30_left" width="144" height="96" srcset="/blog-media/2015/09/1407316419_upload_documents_1600_640-bluesolar-mppt-100-30_left.jpg 144w, /blog-media/2015/09/1407316419_upload_documents_1600_640-bluesolar-mppt-100-30_left.jpg 288w, /blog-media/2015/09/1407316419_upload_documents_1600_640-bluesolar-mppt-100-30_left.jpg 150w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 144px) 100vw, 144px" /></a>bout the 100/30 was the absence of any feedback. Unlike my crappy PWM controller, which gave you a rough-and-ready readout, you plug the BlueSolar in and that&#8217;s it. There&#8217;s no indication of the charge rate or PV output other than three humble LEDs.<img loading="lazy" data-attachment-id="1040" data-permalink="/blog/victron-bluesolar-the-raspberry-pi-having-fun-with-solar/" data-orig-file="/blog-media/2015/09/416694933_894.jpg" data-orig-size="736,490" data-comments-opened="1" data-image-meta="{&quot;aperture&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;credit&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;camera&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;caption&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;created_timestamp&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;copyright&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;focal_length&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;iso&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;shutter_speed&quot;:&quot;0&quot;,&quot;title&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;orientation&quot;:&quot;0&quot;}" data-image-title="416694933_894" data-image-description="" data-image-caption="" data-large-file="/blog-media/2015/09/416694933_894.jpg" class=" wp-image-1040 alignright" src="/blog-media/2015/09/416694933_894.jpg" alt="416694933_894" width="174" height="116" srcset="/blog-media/2015/09/416694933_894.jpg 300w, /blog-media/2015/09/416694933_894.jpg 174w, /blog-media/2015/09/416694933_894.jpg 348w, /blog-media/2015/09/416694933_894.jpg 150w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 174px) 100vw, 174px" /></p>
<p>My cunning plan was to use watt-meters, They are pretty good and plentiful on eBay, but &#8211; and yes there&#8217;s a but &#8211; they don&#8217;t particularly appreciate the PV fluctuations. After two displays blowing up, I called it a day. That said, as good as they were, they lacked one major benefit, remote metrics; You have no idea how your array is doing unless you are sitting with the readouts.</p>
<p>What a great opportunity to dig out the Raspberry Pi? It turns out the BlueSolar provides a standard TTL level serial port, that means it&#8217;s trivial to hook up an MCU. I cheated a bit and bought the <a href="http://www.victronenergy.com/accessories/ve-direct-to-usb-interface">VE.Direct USB cable</a>.</p>
<p>Following a bit more poking around I spotted the <a href="https://www.google.co.uk/url?sa=t&amp;rct=j&amp;q=&amp;esrc=s&amp;source=web&amp;cd=1&amp;cad=rja&amp;uact=8&amp;ved=0CCQQFjAAahUKEwjvwojtzODHAhUFOBQKHUNZDYo&amp;url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.victronenergy.com%2Fupload%2Fdocuments%2FVE.Direct-Protocol.pdf&amp;usg=AFQjCNE3gVI12DypgzuNNmMPg-qArpjFDQ&amp;sig2=OiwrBSTmTCN09QWrIiE-HA&amp;bvm=bv.102022582,d.d24">VE.Direct protocol</a>. Victron clearly have keen developers working for them and they&#8217;ve appreciated how useful a simple protocol is. Despite how closed the industry can be Victron have provided a protocol which is easy to understand, and more importantly, parse!</p>
<p>If you&#8217;re not aware, <a href="https://www.google.co.uk/url?sa=t&amp;rct=j&amp;q=&amp;esrc=s&amp;source=web&amp;cd=1&amp;cad=rja&amp;uact=8&amp;ved=0CCIQFjAAahUKEwi6lIr2z-DHAhVGOxoKHc2_BPw&amp;url=https%3A%2F%2Fgithub.com%2Frxtx%2Frxtx&amp;usg=AFQjCNH_Nkf9fICnjrgdMQa1ZSq-wu-Q7g&amp;sig2=JtYCOyy6tn0SEIu2v-RFXw">RXTX </a>is an excellent library which let&#8217;s a JVM communicate with an Serial device. As a Scala developer. I felt Akka would be a<br />
great companion to RXTX and it would appear <a href="https://github.com/msiegenthaler/rxtx-akka-io">I&#8217;m not the only one</a>.</p>
<p style="text-align:left;"><a href="https://www.google.co.uk/url?sa=t&amp;rct=j&amp;q=&amp;esrc=s&amp;source=web&amp;cd=1&amp;cad=rja&amp;uact=8&amp;ved=0CCQQFjAAahUKEwjX7ZzQ0uDHAhXFVRQKHV5vCog&amp;url=https%3A%2F%2Fthingspeak.com%2F&amp;usg=AFQjCNGv6eNcqr13B69C_yllPWXWp7dFmA&amp;sig2=8dWDX62xEH37LWTiqdg-JQ&amp;bvm=bv.102022582,d.d24">ThinkSpeak </a>is pretty handy. They provide a simple tool which lets you graph any sort of <a href="https://www.google.co.uk/url?sa=t&amp;rct=j&amp;q=&amp;esrc=s&amp;source=web&amp;cd=6&amp;cad=rja&amp;uact=8&amp;ved=0CFoQFjAFahUKEwjdpsjs0eDHAhVBNxQKHZ23DVI&amp;url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FInternet_of_Things&amp;usg=AFQjCNFIwGxmLyqzOcQ7attmuNoZJ8_Jhg&amp;sig2=WFigsZip_d5lR1gMsahufg">IoT </a>metrics. While not quite what I was after, it didn&#8217;t take long before I <a href="https://thingspeak.com/channels/53802">saw the numbers roll in</a></p>
