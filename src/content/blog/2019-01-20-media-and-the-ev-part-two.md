<p><em>(This article was originally written for a local publication)</em></p>
<p><span style="font-weight:400;">The media have performed an excellent job casting the electric vehicle as nothing more than an inconvenience yet this couldn&#8217;t be further from the truth, modern EVs are almost indistinguishable from normal cars bar several key difference, torque, charging,maintenance and finally, Silence (with a capital S).</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight:400;">There’s nothing comparable to an EV accelerating off the line, unlike internal combustion cars (ICE) torque is immediately available at all rev ranges, this translates into show stopping, almost supercar like acceleration, overtaking maneuvers become far safer and is always guaranteed to put a smile on your face. With no gears you can creep along in traffic, stop and start with no noisy engine ignitions, and when it is time to put the pedal to the metal, pure and constant acceleration is delivered thanks to the gearless drivetrain.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight:400;">Let&#8217;s look at charging, as that&#8217;s always a hot topic. If you own an EV you&#8217;ve got to know your ABCs &#8211; Always Be Charging. When you have an opportunity to charge, whether that be at home, work, shopping etc &#8211; plug in. Depending on your car, and charger type you&#8217;ll get anywhere from 100-350 miles off range in an hour.  What&#8217;s more, a significant number are free to use &#8211; I write to you now sitting in the Lidl parking lot in Bexleyheath not having paid a penny…. Ok, ok I had to buy a pint of milk. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight:400;">One huge benefit of EVs is you can leave your car charging overnight meaning you wake up to a full tank of petrol, well electricity, but you get my meaning; In fact there’s a significant population of EV drivers who simply never use public chargers.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight:400;">Statistically speaking in the US the average daily distance driven is less than 30 miles. If your battery range is 2-300 miles, it’ll only be on rare occasions you need to use the public charge network. Whilst this may not suite the travelling salesmen of old, for the majority of us it&#8217;s ideal.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight:400;">Now it&#8217;s not all sunshine and roses, whilst there are tens of thousands of chargers around the country we will need vastly more &#8211; it&#8217;s not much fun waiting for a charger to free up! EV owners can even be their own worst enemy by overstaying at a charge bay despite a fully battery; Thankfully the community have come up with two solutions, charge sharing by allowing other drivers to unplug your car, and at more disputed locations, an idle charge to encourage a prompt return for the car. It&#8217;s also becoming increasingly common to see drivers prioritise the cars with the least range to charge first which is great to see in action.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight:400;">Another frustrating aspect of charging is the sheer number of different providers, each with their own phone apps, cards and subscriptions. Thankfully the tide is quickly changing as the government are pushing for simple contactless payments &#8211; already offered at many stations </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight:400;">Faulty chargers and ICEing bring this section to a neat close. Unfortunately it is not uncommon to find a faulty charger &#8211; thankfully the excellent EV community built ZapMap, a crowd-sourced website &amp; mobile app that gives you the real-time status of all EV chargers throughout the UK. With a bit of forethought you can generally avoid wasting time at faulty or busy chargers.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight:400;">ICEing &#8211; the deliberate action of parking in an EV charger by a normal car &#8211; is a particularly irritating event, akin to parking your car at a petrol pump and going off to do the weekly shop.  Often this appears to be out of malice, or some sort of entitlement but thankfully it is reasonable rare. Most chargers tend to be placed with ANPR systems which will automatically issue a ticket if the vehicle is not parked in the correct location.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight:400;">With that out of the way, let’s talk maintenance or more precisely, there lack of! An EV has vastly less moving parts than a typically car which drastically reduces the number of components that need maintenance or can fail. Your yearly service involves topping up the washer fluid, wiper blades, maybe a coolant check if you’re a high miler and a quick check of the 12v battery &#8211; annual service intervals become almost pointless. Brake pads tend to last significantly longer as regenerative braking &#8211; braking that is provided by the motor and simultaneously recharges the battery &#8211; means it’s entirely possible to drive without using the brake at all! Simply put, the majority of service requirements can be carried out at home saving you a small fortune each year.</span></p>
