<p class="wp-block-paragraph">It’ll be no surprise to anyone that energy prices across the board have gone through the roof, along with just about everything else much to the enrichment of the oil and gas producers who are now <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/business/2022/oct/27/profits-at-worlds-seven-biggest-oil-firms-soar-to-almost-150bn-this-year-windfall-tax">reporting record profits</a>. Following the age-old play book, rather than focus on the source of the problem, businesses and government are pushing the responsibility to solve this crisis on to the end consumer &#8211; you and I. We’ve seen this before with <a href="https://www.npr.org/2020/09/11/897692090/how-big-oil-misled-the-public-into-believing-plastic-would-be-recycled">recycling </a>(tl;dr it’s cheaper to create new plastic, than truly recycle), <a href="https://harvardpolitics.com/climate-change-responsibility/">climate crisis</a> (the majority of emissions come from fewer than 100 organisations), <a href="https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/deutsche-banks-dws-sued-by-consumer-group-over-alleged-greenwashing-2022-10-24/">greenwashing </a>and many other examples.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Some years ago the European Union required all member states to <a href="https://ses.jrc.ec.europa.eu/smart-metering-deployment-european-union">roll out a number of initiatives</a>; one of which was the ‘smart-meter’. Briefly, this new generation of meter has the capability of reporting back your energy consumption as frequently as the infrastructure allows. It also has a few secondary functions which are dubious in nature but we’ll come back to that later.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Where this becomes relevant is the government&#8217;s initiative to become <a href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/net-zero-strategy">net zero by 2050</a>. At the time of writing Chancellor Hunt announced the consumer should <a href="https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-politics-63738388"><em>“cut their energy to stop the UK being ‘blacklackmailed”</em></a>. Of course these are lofty goals we should aim to achieve as soon as possible, but is it actually for the country&#8217;s betterment, or another deflection onto the consumer?</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Given the chaotic nature of the energy market, many families are struggling as a result, the government and industry have identified a <a href="https://www.nationalgrideso.com/news/national-grid-eso-and-octopus-energy-launch-trial-unleash-demand-flexibility-winter">perfect opportunity to trial</a> the appetite for consumer load shifting and reduction. Essentially incentivising electricity customers to reduce or entirely shut off power for a brief period in peak periods. Where a reduction is made against a benchmark (ie. your typical use) a credit is made against your bil &#8211; <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/business/2022/oct/20/national-grid-to-pay-households-more-to-use-off-peak-power">allegedly rising to a stratospheric £3/kWh</a> if that load can be moved to an off peak period. This all sounds great, but clearly utilities, The National Grid and the Government are testing the water. We already know there are proposals to utilise ‘smart’ appliances, including vehicles which will be offered the choice to automatically reschedule their consumption to a period of the National Grid’s choosing. My concern is when will this no longer be a choice?&nbsp;</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Back to smart meter for a moment. There’s two darker aspects which they enable, or at best make a lot easiest to perform. The first and most alarming is the ability to remotely cut off supply, a supply that may be vital for heating, cooking, transport or medical reasons. Now to be transparent, this happens to a degree already, and has done for decades through prepayment metres. Once your credit runs out (and I remember the days of the coin operated 50 pence models) your power shuts off. Whilst I’m not proposing companies giving away free energy, suppliers do need to consider the implications of cutting supply suddenly. Given recent events, I don’t believe they do. If you are not aware utility suppliers have <a href="https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/news/2022/11/smart-meter-prepayment-switched-remotely-rights/">remotely switched credit meter which fell into arrears to prepayment without notification</a>. Quite worrying if they are ignorant of a customer&#8217;s circumstances.</p>



<p class="wp-block-paragraph">In summary my message is: Are customers being made the scapegoat for failings of governments and big business? Will credit meter move from a mechanism for a consumer to keep track of their consumption to another form of energy enforcement? Could encouraging consumers to move consumption to dynamic off peak periods save us money, or is it another form of control? </p>
